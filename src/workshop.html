09.1 Anti-thetis · Leg

A knee that knows how to walk.

The full-leg exo adds thigh cuffs and this hinge above the Kicker. Two 45×55×6 bearings carry the shin side around the thigh block, one revolute joint, nothing clever. The clever part is what a knee actually does in a stride.

Press Walk and the hinge follows a textbook gait-lab curve for level walking. The knee is almost straight when your heel lands, flexes about 18° to absorb the hit, straightens again, then whips to about 60° so your toes clear the floor in swing.

Hinge geometry is from Hinge v47 in Fusion. The curve is an average adult profile, not a measurement of me.

09.2 Scooter · HTD 3M

A knee scooter with a motor where the wheel should be.

Knee scooters are slow on purpose. This one has powered rear wheels, a throttle on the bars, and a basket because it still has to be useful. The Fusion folders show it went through GT2 and HTD 3M belt revisions on the way.

Steer it. The front axle swings on the steering joint, and with a 51 cm wheelbase the turning radius falls straight out of the geometry:

R = L / tan δ L = 51.2 cm (rear axle to front axle in the model), δ = steering angle

Model: Scooter Asm v4. Wheels, steering and the knee-height slider all move on their Fusion joints.

09.3 Couch Surfing

A couch that drives sideways.

It is a full-size sofa on four swerve modules. Each corner has its own drive wheel and its own steering motor, so the couch can translate in any direction and spin at the same time, like an office chair with opinions.

Drive it. Every module solves one line of vector math: its wheel has to move at the couch's velocity plus the spin's contribution at that corner.

vi = v + ω × ri v = couch velocity, ω = spin rate, ri = corner position from the centre

Drag on the driveway to drive, or use WASD and Q/E. Leave it alone and it shows off. Red arrows are wheels running backwards: past 90° it is quicker to reverse a wheel than to steer it all the way round. The inset is the real steering hub from Fusion, turned by the front-left module.

09.4 Cat Turret

Two servos and a laser, versus one cat.

A pan servo, a tilt servo on top of it, and a laser module on the tilt horn. That is the entire machine. Two angles are enough to point anywhere in front of it.

Move your cursor over it. Then try to make it stop.

find pan, tilt so that beam(pan, tilt) ∥ (target − barrel) The pan axis in this design leans 45°, so there is no tidy atan2. The page solves both angles numerically every frame.

Model: Cat Turret v4. Both servo joints are its Fusion revolutes. On a phone, tap and drag.

09.5 Scooter · accessories

Coffee, on crutches.

On crutches you lose both hands, which means you lose your coffee. This is a printed helical holder that clamps between the two rails of a standard aluminium crutch.

Swing the crutch. The cup tilts with it, but the coffee surface doesn't: gravity sets the surface, not the crutch, so all it can do is slosh. Swing hard enough and the slosh wins.

Drag the crutch to swing it yourself. The surface tilt is first-order physics (tangential acceleration over g) plus a toy slosh, not a fluid simulation.
